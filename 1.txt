Building a GPU shader art framework that transforms realistic bacterial growth into psychedelic, neon art requires combining **biological differential equations**, **GPU simulation architectures**, **3D heightmap lighting**, and **optical shader effects** [1-4].

Here is a breakdown of the key systems needed to code this in GLSL/HLSL or WebGPU.

---

### 1. The Core Simulation Engine: GPU Reaction-Diffusion
At the heart of realistic colony expansion is the **Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion system** [5-8]. It models two virtual chemicals: substrate \\(A\\) (nutrient feed) and catalyst \\(B\\) (expanding bacteria) [5, 7, 9].

* **The Differential Equations** [5, 8, 10]:
  \\[\frac{\partial A}{\partial t} = D_A \nabla^2 A - AB^2 + F(1 - A)\\]
  \\[\frac{\partial B}{\partial t} = D_B \nabla^2 B + AB^2 - (F + k)B\\]
* **GPU Ping-Pong Architecture**: Fragment shaders cannot read from and write to the same texture simultaneously [11-13]. Two floating-point textures (`T_ping` and `T_pong`) are used to read the previous frame's chemical concentrations, compute the update, write to the secondary texture, and swap roles each pass [11, 12, 14, 15].
* **Discretizing the Laplacian (\\(\nabla^2\\))**: The diffusion term \\(\nabla^2\\) measures spatial spread across adjacent grid pixels [16]. It is calculated in GLSL using a 9-point convolution stencil over neighboring texels [10, 16, 17]:
  ```glsl
  vec2 lap = -1.0 * current;
  lap += 0.20 * (texRight + texLeft + texUp + texDown);
  lap += 0.05 * (texUpRight + texUpLeft + texDownRight + texDownLeft);
  ```

### 2. Morphotype Parameter Space & Domain Warping
The morphology of the colony is governed by the **Feed Rate (\\(F\\))** and **Kill Rate (\\(k\\))** [5, 18-20]:

* **Parameter Regimes (\\(F, k\\))** [18, 21-23]:
  * **Mitosis & Cell Division**: \\(F = 0.0545, k = 0.062\\) [24, 25]
  * **Solitons & Coral Spots**: \\(F = 0.030, k = 0.062\\) [22, 23]
  * **Labyrinthine Mazes**: \\(F = 0.029, k = 0.057\\) [22]
  * **Pulsating Spirals & Waves**: \\(F = 0.014, k = 0.047\\) [21, 22]
* **Psychedelic Fluid Swirls (Domain Warping)**: To make the growth look organic and liquid rather than strictly uniform, feed an underlying **Fractal Brownian Motion (fBm)** noise field or vector field into the coordinate lookups [26, 27]. Warping the texture UV space (\\(\vec{u} \to \vec{u} + \mathbf{fBm}(\vec{u})\\)) causes branching tendrils to twist into swirling, psychedelic tendrils [27].

### 3. Converting 2D Concentrations into 3D Embossed Heights
To give the 2D chemical density map a tangible, 3D organic texture, treat concentration \\(B(x,y)\\) as a surface heightmap [1, 27, 28]:

* **Finite-Difference Surface Normals**: Compute 3D normal vectors \\(\mathbf{N}\\) directly in the fragment shader by sampling adjacent pixel concentrations [1, 3, 29]:
  ```glsl
  float hL = texture(chemTex, uv - vec2(texel.x, 0.0)).g;
  float hR = texture(chemTex, uv + vec2(texel.x, 0.0)).g;
  float hD = texture(chemTex, uv - vec2(0.0, texel.y)).g;
  float hU = texture(chemTex, uv + vec2(0.0, texel.y)).g;
  
  vec3 normal = normalize(vec3((hL - hR) * heightScale, (hD - hU) * heightScale, 1.0));
  ```
* **Specular Wet Gloss**: Applying Cook-Torrance or Phong specular lighting to the reconstructed normal vectors gives the biofilm a moist, reflective sheen [1, 3, 30, 31].

### 4. Trippy Neon Optics & Shading Effects

```
[ Simulation Grid (A, B) ] 
          │
          ├──> Normal Reconstruction (Finite Differences) ──> Specular & Wet Gloss
          ├──> Cosine Gradient Palette (Inigo Quilez) ─────> Electric Neon Colors
          ├──> Subsurface Scattering (Screen-Space Blur) ─> Translucent Gel Glow
          └──> Fresnel Edge Calculation ──────────────────> Intense Rim Highlights
```

1. **Cosine Color Palettes (Inigo Quilez Method)** [3]:
   Map chemical concentration values \\(B(x,y)\\) to spectral neon gradients using trigonometric color curves [3, 32]:
   \\[\mathbf{Color}(B) = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} \cdot \cos(2\pi(\mathbf{c} \cdot B + \mathbf{d}))\\]
   By modulating phase vectors \\(\mathbf{d}\\), the colony dynamically shifts through toxic cyans, hot magentas, electric blues, and lime greens as it grows [3, 32].
2. **Subsurface Scattering (SSS)** [3, 33, 34]:
   Biological tissue diffuses light internally [33, 34]. Applying a screen-space Gaussian diffusion profile to the concentration map simulates light penetrating and scattering inside gelatinous cell clusters, producing an illuminated, inner glow [3, 33, 35, 36].
3. **Fresnel & Rim Glow** [3, 37]:
   Calculate view-dependent rim lighting (\\(\text{pow}(1.0 - |\mathbf{N} \cdot \mathbf{V}|, p)\\)) so that steep 3D edges, wrinkles, and advancing growth fronts emit an intense neon outline [3, 37].

### 5. Interactive & Audio Modulation
* **Audio-Reactive Controls**: Map incoming live audio FFT spectrum bands to simulation uniforms [38, 39]:
  * **Bass Frequencies**: Modulate feed rates (\\(F\\)) or inject catalyst \\(B\\) at random coordinates to trigger new growth seeds on drum beats [10, 39].
  * **Treble Frequencies**: Modulate diffusion rates (\\(D_A, D_B\\)) or lighting rim intensity to make the edges pulse in time with high notes [3, 18, 39].
